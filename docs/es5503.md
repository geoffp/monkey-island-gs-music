# The Ensoniq DOC (ES5503): references and hardware facts

The IIgs plays NinjaTrackerPlus songs through the Ensoniq 5503 Digital Oscillator Chip (DOC).
This file indexes the references we keep and the facts we've taken from them, so emulator
behaviour can be checked against the chip rather than against another emulator.

## Facts

- **The stock IIgs is mono.** "You can connect stereo headphones to this jack, [but] you'll hear
  only monophonic sound: identical sounds from both channels" (TIL #2391). The DOC's channel
  bits (control register CA0–CA3) only drive an external demultiplexer on J25; stereo cards
  decode CA0, with odd channels on the left (TN #19, Hardware Reference fig. 5-10). So a stock
  machine sums every oscillator into one signal. **KEGS** 1.38 also ignores the channel bits and
  mixes everything into one signal (`doc.c` `doc_play`). **GSSquared** plays stereo-card output
  with a mono-mirroring heuristic (docs/ninjatrackerplus.md).
- **Stereo cards chop, then filter.** Apple's reference demultiplexer (Hardware Reference
  fig. 5-10) switches the J25 waveform with an MC14052, addressed by CSTRB and CA0, into two
  inverting stages that feed low-pass filters. It has no hold capacitor, so each side gets the
  signal during its own oscillators' slots and ground otherwise. The csa2 FAQ's 4-channel decoder
  ("the same technique as stereo cards") is the same: CMOS switches, then a 17.7 kHz active
  low-pass. Either way, each oscillator's weight on its side is its duty share, equal for all,
  which is the linear sum the emulators compute. "Most stereo cards (AE's Sonic Blaster, for
  example) only pay attention to the lowest bit" (csa2 FAQ). The **Audio Animator**'s own
  circuit isn't documented: its manual describes only the box. That box has a 1 W PHONES
  amplifier with Output Volume bars and software-set left/right volume bars. The left/right
  bars affect PHONES but not Line Out.
- **NTP pans hard.** Each NTP track is on channel 0 or 1 only (`ntpconverter_lib.php`
  `$doc_stereo_defaults` 1 0 0 1 …; the player keeps bits 4–6 of the header byte, so CA0 alone).
  With a stereo card, a track comes out of one side only. A mono IIgs sums the two.
- **Volume is applied in analog.** "The output section of the DOC consist[s] of two cascaded
  eight bit Digital to Analog Converters (DACs) … The upper DAC contains the volume and the lower
  DAC contains the waveform information" (ERS p. 2–3). The data sheet and Hardware Reference
  only say that the sample is "multiplied by the 8-bit volume". Every emulator we have (MAME,
  KEGS, GSSquared, a2fpga) models that as an exact linear product. How linear the real DAC is
  at low volume codes isn't documented anywhere we've found.
- **One DAC, time-shared.** Each oscillator gets one DOC cycle per scan, plus two refresh cycles.
  The motherboard filters the multiplexed output, which integrates the oscillators (ERS p. 3).
- **The uppermost enabled oscillator counts three times** on real silicon (MAME header,
  emulated since 2.3). NTP's interrupt oscillator is #31, the highest, at volume 0
  (`ninjatrackerplus.s` `interrupt_oscillator`), so this doesn't touch our tracks.
- **Swap-mode anomaly (TN #11).** A swap from the odd oscillator back to the even one
  outputs centreline ($80) for one sample period, which clicks. Even-to-odd swaps don't.
  NTP's 2-oscillator instruments swap even (attack) → odd (loop), and the loop doesn't swap
  back, so this shouldn't touch our loops. MAME's header puts the 0x80 cycle on every swap.
- **Pointer bits per table size** (ERS p. 6): a table of 2^n bytes uses only the pointer bits
  above it, and a table can't wrap past the top of DOC RAM.

## Open: real IIgs vs. emulators (2026-11-14)

User report from a real IIgs: the **Panpipes** in MI160, the **sustained notes** in MI159
("I think they're chorus": Chorale) and the **melodies** in MI162 (BottleBlow) are "almost
inaudible", where both emulators play them clearly. Measured from the per-song MODs: these
parts play at DOC volume 16–40 (Chorale 24–28 median, BottleBlow 24–40, Panpipes 40 at 44),
under bass and drums at 100–255. Panpipes 68/75 in MI160 are louder (about 90–120). Every one
of them is a **sustained, 2-oscillator swap-mode** instrument (a ~0.1 s attack, then a loop).
Candidate causes, none confirmed:

1. the real volume DAC is quieter than linear at low codes;
2. the loop oscillator doesn't start, or starts silent, on real hardware, so only the attack
   is heard;
3. ~~mono summing masks quiet parts that stereo separates~~: the listening machine has an
   **Applied Engineering Audio Animator**, so it plays in stereo, like GSSquared;
4. a left/right imbalance on the card: unlikely, because the flagged instruments play on both
   sides about equally (MI160 Panpipes 75: 87 notes left, 75 right; MI162 BottleBlow 62: 28 / 22;
   MI159 Chorale 67: 21 / 7, Chorale 59: 3 / 14).

If the Audio Animator chops and filters like Apple's reference circuit, its stereo has the same
relative levels as GSSquared's. That leaves 1 and 2, both in the DOC itself.

**Independent hardware evidence for 1 (Apple2Infinitum Slack, #CABEM8JFK, thread of
2026-11-12, pasted by the user 2026-11-14).** Ian Brumby (author of SWAPTEST, which MAME's swap
rules come from) measured the DOC on real IIgs machines, with analogue voltage measurements
(volume) and a logic analyser. He scores emulators against those measurements in a report card,
`Ensoniq.html`, posted in the thread; the test program is to be released. His findings:
- "Volume overall is non-linear, and each oscillator is affected by the surrounding
  oscillators (with oscillator 32 being 3 times as impacted)."
- A second user's symptom matches ours: in synthLAB, "the background horns are very prominent
  in emulation, while on the real GS, they are the correct, almost inaudible volume". The same
  user suspects this is why a Doom port's music sounds off on real hardware. The emulator
  Crossrunner's released version has the same fault; its in-development version fixes it.
  Crossrunner is Ian Brumby's own emulator (announced on comp.emulators.apple2, 2023-12-27;
  issue tracker `github.com/ibrumby/Crossrunner-Issues`). So the report card grades his
  emulator against his own measurements; its volume model is the one working implementation of
  the curve, and its beta is a way to hear the songs as hardware plays them.
- MAME and KEGS score similarly, but on different items. Many newer emulators derive from MAME
  (GSSquared's DOC began as MAME's `es5503`).
- Ian Brumby's `DOCGRADE.po` collects hardware readings: run it at 2.8 MHz, note ROM01 or ROM03
  and a cold or warm machine. It writes results back to the disk (up to 9 runs). On his ROM03
  two Sound GLU tests fail; his ROM01 passes them all.

So the emulators' linear `sample × volume` is known to be wrong, and quiet oscillators come out
louder in emulation than on hardware: that is our report.

**The report card (`Ensoniq.html`, pasted by the user 2026-11-14; not kept here).** 64
behaviours measured on a ROM 01 IIgs with probe disks, scored across 17 emulators and FPGA cores.
Totals: Crossrunner pre-release 59/64, GSSquared 1.0 30, MiSTer 30, KEGS 1.38 18, MAME 19.
Apple's documents describe 16. Its "Sound output" rows, the ones that matter for us:
- **"The volume register follows the measured curve, silent below 17."** Only Crossrunner does
  this. Apple's documents are marked ✗, so they say otherwise (linear). The curve's values aren't
  in the card.
- **"Each oscillator also plays a share of the previous slot's volume level"** (the neighbour
  effect). Only Crossrunner.
- **"An oscillator's level is in inverse proportion to the count plus two, and the last one has
  three slots."** Crossrunner and ApplEm; MAME and GSSquared do ×3 but ignore the count. NTP
  runs 32 oscillators, so this is a constant for us, apart from oscillator 31 (silent).
- **"The sample converter's bit 7 is worth about 120.5 steps, not 128."** Nobody models it: a
  DAC nonlinearity that bends every waveform slightly.
- **"The system volume scales the output by the measured law."** Nobody models it. NTP writes
  the Control Panel volume into the GLU (`ninjatrackerplus.s` `access_doc_registers`). It acts
  on the motherboard's speaker/headphone path only, not on the DOC output at J25 that stereo
  cards take. The report card's note on Crossrunner: "Alien Mind sets it to 0 to mute the
  speaker when its stereo output is chosen". So with the Audio Animator the Control Panel volume
  doesn't change what we measure. (User, 2026-11-15. KEGS scales everything by it.)
- Odd→even swap drops to zero for a sample (TN #11): Crossrunner yes; GSSquared no.
- GSSquared also fails "one oscillator per slot, in number order", which matches its batching
  (docs/ninjatrackerplus.md: swap loops start up to 256 samples early).

**What it means for our songs.** NTP's DOC volume is MOD volume × 1020/256 (`setplayvolume` at
255), so **MOD volume 4 and under (DOC < 17) is silent on hardware**, and the curve is presumably
far below linear just above that. Share of sounding track-rows (melodic and drum, rest excluded,
releases included) below DOC 17 in `inst-per-song`: 3–28 % per song (MI151 28 %, MI163 27 %,
MI159 26 %, MI162 6 %). MI159 Elec Organ 62: 76 % of its rows; MI160 Acou Bass 51 %, Panpipes 75
35 %, BottleBlow 37 %. The flagged parts' main bodies sit at DOC 24–40, just above the threshold
(Chorale median 24–28, BottleBlow 24–40). A SenseiPlay play volume below 255 would push more under.

**DOCGRADE on the listening machine (user's runs, 2026-11-14, `ref/DOCGRADE.po`).** Ensoniq
Test Suite v1.1 (DOCGRADE 1.2) has 70 digital tests in seven groups: Sound GLU, registers, scan
and oscillator count, modes and starts, wave fetch and DATA, interrupts, power-on. It has **no
sound-output tests**: it runs silently and doesn't measure the volume curve. Three runs failed:
G5 ($C03F change redirects a step before $C03E), G6 (adjacent $C03D accesses: write replaces,
read ignored), G8 (replaced read leaves the DOC's fetch in the latch), G9 (access after a
register write goes to its register), S6 (count changed late: the scan jumps ahead) and S7
(oscillators outside the scan drift), all three times. G7 (mode change at a write's slot loses
the write) failed in runs 2 and 3, and R3 (power-on $E0 names an oscillator left unscanned) in
run 3 only. Ian Brumby's ROM01 passes every test; his ROM03 fails two GLU tests. So the
GLU behaves differently between machines, and Ian wants these runs, with the ROM version and
whether the machine was cold or warm. None of these failures touches the volume path. The G
tests are back-to-back $C03D timing edge cases. NTP writes registers through the GLU, but at
ordinary spacing as far as we know (unchecked).

**Measuring the curve ourselves: the DOC volume sweep (2026-11-14).** `just docsweep` builds
`build/docsweep/DOCSWEEP.PO`: SenseiPlay plus a test song (`src/migs/docsweep.py`, layout in
its docstring). A 453 Hz tone on oscillator 0 steps through MOD volumes 64 → 0 (every DOC
volume NTP can produce), about 1 s each. A constant 1424 Hz pilot on oscillator 9 is the level
reference, so gain changes after the DOC cancel. Section B checks the neighbour effect (a
712 Hz tone on the oscillator before or after a quiet test tone); section C plays a
half-amplitude table, to tell a threshold on the register from one on sample × volume.
`doc-sweep-analyse REC.wav build/docsweep/DOCSWEEP.json --csv out.csv` measures a recording.
Baselines, both linear as expected:
- desktop (libopenmpt): within ±0.2 dB of linear (NTP's volume rounding); noise floor −107 dB;
- GSSquared 1.0 (`just emu-docsweep`, PipeWire capture of its output, 48 kHz): within
  −0.3…+1.2 dB of linear, so the top few steps are about 1 dB compressed (GSSquared's mix or
  the capture, not looked into); no neighbour effect (±0.6 dB); half table −6 dB; noise floor
  −49 dB. Rows measured at 119.5 ms (nominal 120).
SenseiPlay keys through gs2-mcp need `hold_s` ≈ 0.15 s; 0.02 s presses are often missed.

**Measured on the listening machine (2026-11-15).** The user's IIgs, Audio Animator Line Out →
small digital mixer → GarageBand (iOS), 44.1 kHz 24-bit, peak 0.99 with no clipping. Recording
`measurements/docsweep/2026-11-15-iigs-audio-animator.flac` (lossless, as recorded), with the
step list it was played with; `just doc-curve` writes `config/doc-volume.toml` (the build's curve)
and `build/docsweep/curve.csv` and `doc-volume-curve.html` from it. Pilot and test at 1422 / 452.5 Hz (0.4 % below GSSquared's); rows
119.97 ms; noise floor −77 dB below the pilot.
- **The volume curve (section A).** Level relative to a linear DOC, with DOC 255 as 0 dB:

  | DOC | 255 | 191 | 159 | 127 | 95 | 79 | 63 | 47 | 39 | 31 | 27 | 23 | 19 | 15…3 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | vs linear, dB | 0 | −0.6 | −1.1 | −1.7 | −2.6 | −3.5 | −4.8 | −7.3 | −9.7 | −14.4 | −19.7 | −31.8 | −30.2 | −24…−11 |
  | below full scale, dB | 0 | −3.1 | −4.4 | −7.7 | −11.2 | −13.7 | −16.8 | −21.9 | −25.8 | −32.5 | −38.9 | −52.4 | −52.4 | −48.5…−47.6 |

  Above about DOC 40 it is close to linear in (volume − 28): the chip acts as if it subtracts
  about 28 from the register. Below, it bends down to a **null at DOC 19–23 (−52 dB)**. Under that,
  DOC 3–15 all give the **same small output, −48 dB**, which halves with a half-amplitude table, so
  it's signal, not noise. That points to a constant leak of opposite sign, cancelling the volume
  term near DOC 20. Register 0 is silent (−84 dB). This agrees with Ian Brumby's "silent below
  17": nothing at or under DOC 23 is audible in a mix. Here the audible floor for NTP is **DOC 27
  (MOD 7)**.
- **The threshold is on the register, not sample × volume (section C).** The half-amplitude table
  is 6 dB down at every volume (−6.4, −6.1, −6.0, −5.8, and −5.2…−6.2 in the floor). So the
  curve is a function of the volume register alone.
- **The neighbour effect is real and one-directional (section B).** A loud oscillator in the
  slot **before** raises a quiet one; one in the slot **after** doesn't (±0.3 dB):

  | test (osc 2) | alone | prev. osc DOC 63 | 127 | 255 | next osc DOC 255 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | DOC 23 | −37.5 | −30.7 | −25.8 | **−24.7 (+12.8 dB)** | −37.2 |
  | DOC 39 | −18.7 | −17.5 | −16.0 | −15.7 (+3.0) | −18.4 |
  | DOC 79 | −6.9 | −6.5 | −6.1 | −6.3 (+0.6) | −6.6 |

  On section A's curve that is about **+5 to +10 on the effective volume** (DOC 23 with a full
  neighbour sounds like DOC 33; DOC 39 like 47; DOC 79 like 84), so it matters only for quiet
  oscillators. (Section A's test tone is on oscillator 0, whose previous slots are refresh and
  oscillator 31, silent in NTP, so A is the curve without help.)
- **Compensation for NTP:** the MOD volume m′ whose measured level matches a linear m/64 is
  **m′ ≈ 7 + m × 57/64** (rounded: 1→7, 2→9, 4→11, 6→13, 8→15, 12→19, 16→23, 24→30, 32→37,
  48→51, 64→64; within 0.3 dB from m = 2 up, and MOD 1 can only reach −38.9 dB against −36.1).
  So it adds ~7 at the quiet end, nothing at the top, and the dynamic range survives.
- **Why the flagged parts vanish:** Chorale (MI159) at DOC 24–28 and BottleBlow (MI160/162) at
  24–40 play 20–30 dB below their linear level. MI159's Elec Organ 62 at DOC 16 is in the floor.
  A loud oscillator just before them helps by chance.
- **A shareable report:** `uv run doc-sweep-report build/docsweep/realgs.csv build/docsweep/doc-volume-curve.html`
  writes one self-contained HTML page (data and drawing code inline; light and dark): the curve
  against linear, the gap, the neighbour effect, the table and the method (`src/migs/docsweep_report.py`).
- One machine and one card, ROM unknown. Ian's "below 17" against our null at 19–23 may be the
  NTP step size (no DOC 16–18) or a difference between chips.

**Fix options, needing the curve** (from Ian Brumby, the Crossrunner beta, or our own
measurement of a Line Out recording):
1. a hardware volume map in `ntpmod`: pick the DOC volume whose *measured* level matches the
   wanted level, as a build variant (emulators would then play quiet parts too loud);
2. at least a floor: keep a sounding note at DOC ≥ 17 (MOD ≥ 5) and end releases with the rest
   sample at that level, not by fading through the silent band;
3. narrow the dynamic range (normalise the loudest moment lower than 64 and lift the quiet
   end), which costs the loud end little on a nonlinear curve.
Our docsim, loop and level measures all assume a linear DOC, so they misjudge quiet parts the
same way.

A test on the real machine will tell them apart: solo the part, and step its volume.
